# Workshop flow and content handling

## Default workshop arc

Start from the requested outcome, not a slide count. A common teacher-workshop sequence is:

1. Arrival and orientation — why this matters now and what will happen.
2. Starting point — a short poll, question, or recognisable classroom tension when useful.
3. Concrete outcome — show a real result before a long explanation.
4. Core idea — explain only the concepts needed to understand or use the outcome.
5. Guided demonstration — let participants watch one meaningful action or decision unfold.
6. Try it — provide a bounded first action, download, copyable prompt, or practice start.
7. Extend and share — optional variants, resources, Q&A, or peer reflection.
8. Closing — name the next small action and how participants can get help.

This is a starting arc, not a required list. Compress, expand, reorder, or omit sections to fit the teaching objective and time.

## Content lanes

Separate the material into lanes before creating slides.

| Lane | What belongs here | Where it appears |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teaching spine | Goal, claim, participant decision, transition | Slide map and visible deck |
| Evidence | Official source, real project, screenshot, demonstration data | Case slide, source note, presenter note |
| Facilitation | What to say, ask, wait for, and do if time is short | Speaker notes or companion guide |
| Practice | Instructions, starting link, expected output, recovery help | Practice slides and handout links |
| Local details | Host, dates, logo, contact, Wi-Fi, local policy | Central config/data block |

Avoid turning every source note into a visible slide. A slide should make one moment of understanding or action happen; the companion guide protects the broader lesson plan from being lost during compression.

## Case slots

Use a case slot when an authentic example is needed but unavailable. A slot must say what is missing and what would fill it.

```text
CASE SLOT — school or teacher example
Purpose: demonstrate how the idea changes a real lesson or workflow.
Needed from presenter: one public URL, approved screenshot, or a 3–5 sentence before/after story.
Fallback: omit this branch; retain the concept and practice prompt.
```

Do not visually disguise a case slot as a completed example. During briefing, ask the user to choose whether to provide the material, allow research, or remove the slot.

## Time design

Reserve time for actions rather than filling all available minutes with explanation. Mark each section as essential, optional, or removable. A long workshop needs planned recovery points; a short workshop needs one complete outcome rather than a tour of every feature.

## Approval boundary

Before substantial implementation, present the workshop map plus: required inputs, unresolved case slots, proposed interactions, and any research/deployment dependency. Revise the structure after user feedback rather than assuming every supplied note is equal-priority slide copy.
